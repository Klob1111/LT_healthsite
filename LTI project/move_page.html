Everyone knows that exercise is good for your body, but how can it change the way you think?

ACT ION

When you exercise, you feel better about yourself. This is probably the first thing that comes to your mind when you think about the correlation of exercise and mental health, but the connection runs deeper. Exercise has shown to come with numerous mental benefits as well, such as improving sleep and decreasing stress tendencies. Exercise helps your brain release neurotransmitters, like serotonin; a chemical that's linked to pleasure and improved sleep quality. All of these benefits are open to people who engage in regular physical activity, which worldwide standards define as at least 150 minutes of a week. The only problem is that this can be a bit daunting for people that consider themselves out of shape, so I've listed a few exercises that are both easily accessible, and can dramatically improve your health. (They're all aerobic exercises, which are proven to work the most benefits.)

Here are a few common exercises:

Walking Walking, while not the most beneficial, is great cardio. If you walk just 30 minutes a day, you would still be hugely improving your exercise habits. It's also a great gateway to other forms of exercise.
Running Running is my personal exercise of choice. You can do it almost anywhere, and it's very effective. Once you start, it can become addicting, and you'll want to get better at it. My personal preference is running outside because it's nice to have a change of scenery.
Biking I will admit, it can be a bit expensive to buy a bike. But if you do, you won't regret it. Biking both acts as a great mode of transport, and a way to exercise. It allows you to explore and see the world while you ride. My suggestion is to go out on a bike and just see where you end up. Maybe you'll discover something new!
Swiming Swimming might not be the most accessible sport, but it's certainly an interesting one. If you have a pool around you and you're comfortable swimming around other people, you'll be rewarded with one of the most engaging hobbies out there. It's nice to make a routine out of it, going to swim laps a few times a week.

Keep in mind while you do these exercises; every bit counts. You don't have to start out running a marathon. Even if you can't hit 150 minutes a week, just 10 would still be beneficial. Studies also suggest that activity done socially during your free time has stronger mental health benefits, so keep this in mind as you schedule.
