Sleep

is a lot more important then you may think...

It's recommended that teens get at least 10 hours of sleep a night. In my experience however, it seems most teens almost never get this amount. I could theorise as to why this is, but whether it's because of academic pressure or the draw of phones, it needs to change. Sleep is especially important for teenagers. Teenhood is one of the most significant times in our lives for brain development, and sleep is when we're growing the most.

The link between mood problems and poor sleep is supported by several studies, with some reporting up to a 55% increase in mood deficits in subjects with poor sleep quality. Furthermore, a positive mood was linked to better sleep, creating a feedback loop. In severe cases, poor sleep can cause sleep disorders, leading to problems with "motor, regulatory, transport, and protective functions of the body, slowing metabolism, and an increased risk of injury or death due to falls or other damage during episodes of sleepwalking or narcolepsy."

Sleep quality is often defined as the depth and length of one's sleep. Not length, but sleep quality seems to be the defining factor in how well rested you are, and, in turn, how well you reap the benefits of “good sleep.” Make sure you're going to bed in a quiet spot, with as few interruptions as possible. Maintaining a consistent sleep schedule can help with this as well, allowing you to fall into a deep sleep faster, so try to go to bed at the same time every night.

Sleep is closely related to a multitude of things, from disorders, to even outlook. It's far and away one of the most important factors in one's life, in terms of mental health. If there's one thing to take away from this site, it's that one more hour of sleep is worth the time loss.
